# R14 – Peer-reviewed evidence for the design

**Drives:** proposals only (E-1..E-9). Nothing in [design.md](../design.md) is changed by this note; every proposal adds a metric, a test or a document line and keeps every spec must as it is.   **Date:** 2026-11-14

**Method.** Papers were found by targeted lookups (Crossref and OpenAlex by title, a few web searches, no Consensus) and kept in PaperLab workspace **"Nyx ground segment design"**, the single Nyx workspace. The 9 alarm papers were copied there from "Nyx alarm management". Companion note: [R15](R15-paper-evidence-enhancements.md), from a parallel session, covers the DLQ/POISON restart loop, chart downsampling, the p95 CI and the error-handler lint. R14 does not repeat those and cites R15 where they meet. Archive/replay integrity and contract testing are left to R16/R17. Only free copies were used: open access, author or institutional pages, arXiv and USENIX. **Full-text claims were read passage by passage with `search_library` / `read_pages`, and the page given is the PDF page.** PaperLab's `verify_claims` was **not** used: its default model is remote (`remote_model_needs_confirm`), and remote use was not allowed. So the verdicts in §4 are my reading of the quoted passage, not a model verdict. "abstract" means only the abstract was read; such rows carry less weight.

## 2. Enhancement proposals

Risk to the core is "none" for every proposal: each one only adds a measurement, a test, a report column or a doc line, and changes no pass condition of the 47.

**E-1 Measured alarm quality (false alarms, chatter, short alarms).** *Problem:* the design limits nuisance alarms by construction (D-ALM-02/03) but never measures them, so "deadband + persistence work" is only asserted. *Evidence:* Ψ (Kondaveeti 2010 p. 3) with the 0.05 alarms/s cut-off (Wang & Chen 2014 p. 3). Ψ can miss chatter, so also apply Rule 3a, T1 or T0 < 20 s (Wang & Chen p. 3–4). False alarms cost more than misses (Dixon 2007, abstract) and train operators to ignore alarms (Bliss 1995, abstract). Spacecraft ops engineers drop tools after "even a couple false positives" (Hundman 2018 p. 8). *Change:* one pure function `gs.alarms.quality(alarm_events, fault_windows) → {raises, false_raises, psi, short_t1, short_t0, peak_10min}` (`false_raises` = RAISE on a parameter with no injected fault affecting it, judged from the simulator ledger / `SIM_SEED` fault log). It is reported (a) in the ATP-01 2 h soak result, expected 0 false raises; (b) as chaos invariant **I8** "0 false raises; no Rule 3a chatter outside injected `step`/`freeze` windows"; (c) as a section of the session report (D-DB-06). The `step` alarms of D-PERF-04 are excluded by design (they are 1 s on purpose). Unit test: `unit/test_alarm_quality.py` on hand-built transition lists (Ψ = 0 for < 2 alarms; Wang & Chen's 7-run-length example gives Ψ < 0.05 but Rule 3a fires). *Strengthens:* ALM-02, ALM-03, ATP-01, GS-EXT-03, DAT-02. *Effort:* M.

**E-2 Deadband / persistence tuning study.** *Problem:* N = 3 and the 15 % cabin-pressure deadband come from the spec (ALM-03) and a floor rule (D-ALM-02). D-ALM-03 cites "the series design in the literature" for the structure, but no study backs the values. *Evidence:* the ROC method needs normal and abnormal data, then picks the smallest delay that meets the false/missed targets (Izadi 2009 p. 6). Its sweeps, delay 2–10 samples and deadband 2–20 % (pp. 5–6), match the proposed grid. Joint design of deadband + timer (Gyasi & Wang 2022 p. 2). FAR/MAR fall and AAD rises with m (Wang & Chen p. 4). A misplaced trip point makes a delay worse (Izadi p. 5). *Change:* offline `perf/alarm_roc.py` (stdlib + the real `gs.alarms` engine, seeded `SIM_SEED`). (1) Nominal runs from §3.3 σ: false RAISE per hour and Ψ. (2) `leak` −0.4 kPa/s and `overheat` profiles: detection delay from the limit crossing to RAISE, and the ALM-04 ROC-before-yellow margin. (3) Cabin-pressure RTN delay after `isolate_valve`. Grid: N = 1..5 × deadband 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15 % for cabin pressure; N = 1..5 alone for the ROC check, which also tests R8's untested claim that persistence filters correlated ROC noise less. Output `docs/perf/alarm-tuning.md` (table + one ROC-style plot per parameter). Acceptance: N = 3 / 15 % is on or near the frontier, with 0 false raises per hour, the ALM-03 spike rejected, the ALM-04 margin ≥ 5 s and an on-delay inside the ALM-05 budget. If a value is dominated, an assumption entry is raised (not a silent change). State the limit: simulator noise is i.i.d. Gaussian, so the study justifies the values for the simulator only. Add to §3.5: "re-run `alarm_roc.py` on recorded bench noise before the first real campaign" (Gyasi and Wang 2019 stress non-i.i.d. signals). *Strengthens:* ALM-02/03/04, D-ALM-02/03 rationale, ATP-02. *Effort:* M.

**E-3 Flood and peak-rate metric (no flood handling built).** *Problem:* the design has no flood handling; R8 says flood control "comes only from per-param aggregation, deadband and persistence". *Evidence:* the flood definition (≥ 10 in 10 min, ending below 5; serious ≥ 100) and the finding that floods are mostly **correct** alarms from propagation (Wang 2016 p. 7). Rationalization lowers the average but not floods (Beebe 2013, abstract). *Change:* add to the session report and to the chaos `summary.md` the **peak new alarms per 10 min** (per vehicle and combined, since one console serves both) and the **time in flood** by the Wang p. 7 definition, which E-1's function computes. Add to §4 "not built": first-out ordering, consequence grouping and flood suppression (Rodrigo 2016, Dorgo 2018), with this metric as the trigger to build them. Correct R8 §5's sentence (§3 below). *Strengthens:* DAT-02, OPS-2, Q14 item 8. *Effort:* S.

**E-4 Partial network partition in the chaos soak.** *Problem:* the chaos actions are kill, pause, restart and broker stop. None cuts one worker off from only Redis or only Postgres, a state where the advisory lock (Postgres) and the consumer `idle` (Redis) disagree. *Evidence:* 29 % of partition failures come from partial partitions, "poorly understood and tested" (Alquraan 2018 pp. 3, 8); 80 % are catastrophic (p. 6); one partition is enough (p. 8). *Change:* a chaos override file `docker-compose.chaos.yml` (core compose untouched) puts Redis and Postgres on separate networks. New action "partition ingest-N from {redis | db}" (`docker network disconnect`, 20–40 s, then reconnect), at most one in flight as today. Expected: Redis-cut → `idle` grows → steal at 30 s → I1–I3 hold. DB-cut → the read loop blocks on the frame transaction → watchdog exit at 30 s (D-ING-26) and/or steal → I1–I3 hold. Add `system/test_partial_partition.py` for both cases. The DB-cut case restarts the worker repeatedly while Postgres is unreachable, so it also exercises R15 E1 (healthy frames counted towards POISON): I6 must stay at `poison = 0` once R15 E1 is in. This complements R15's read-only-role action; it does not replace it. *Strengthens:* ING-01, ING-08, GS-EXT-03, Q8. *Effort:* M.

**E-5 Name the residual fence window (D-ING-12).** *Problem:* the pre-read `SELECT 1` is a **client-side** check. A worker stopped between `SELECT 1` and `XREADGROUP` still pulls entries into its own PEL. They come back only through the 30 s reclaim (late, out of order). Postgres writes are fenced by the server, because they run on the lock session; Redis reads are not. *Evidence:* Chubby, p. 5: a delayed request from a former holder "may be acted on without the protection of L". The **recipient** should check the sequencer, and lock-delay covers recipients that cannot. *Change:* (1) a §3.6 row "fence check-then-read window: entries late by ≤ `RECLAIM_IDLE_MS`, never lost or doubled (D-ING-13/17)"; (2) a hook `STOP_AT=after_fence_before_read` (D-ENG-16) and `system/test_hung_owner_is_stolen[stop_after_fence]` asserting 0 lost, 0 duplicates, out-of-order count reported. This keeps the existing "0 out-of-order" test honest about which window it covers. No mechanism change: a Redis-side fence (WATCH/MULTI or Lua around the read) would drop `BLOCK` and touch a must path. *Strengthens:* ING-01, ING-08, R2-06. *Effort:* S.

**E-6 Error-handler coverage gate (adds to R15 E4).** *Problem:* the 80 % coverage gate (D-ENG-15) can be met while the `except` paths stay unexecuted, and those are the paths that run during the faults this system exists for. R15 E4 adds the lint (`BLE001`, `S110`), which catches the trivial *shape* of a bad handler but not an untested one. *Evidence:* Yuan 2014, OSDI: 92 % of catastrophic failures come from mishandled explicit errors; in 58 %, simple tests of the handlers would have caught the fault (p. 3); coverage of error handlers beats random fault injection (p. 10). *Change:* `tests/meta/test_except_coverage.py`: every `except` block under `src/gs/{ingest,archiver,api}` must be executed by unit or integration tests (line data from `coverage.json`), or be listed in `tests/meta/except_allowlist.txt` with a reason. Ruff `TD`/`FIX` (TODO in handlers, Yuan p. 9) can join R15's rule set. *Strengthens:* ENG-02, D-ING-24, D-ENG-14. *Effort:* S.

**E-7 Percentile sample size and aggregation (adds to R15 E3).** R15 E3 already proposes printing each p95 with its nonparametric 95 % CI (Hoefler & Belli 2015 pp. 4–5); R14 adds two consequences. (1) For p95 a two-sided rank CI exists only from **n ≥ 120** (own calculation, Le Boudec ranks ⌊np − 1.96√(np(1−p))⌋ … ⌈1 + np + 1.96√(np(1−p))⌉: n = 60 → upper rank 62 > n; n = 120 → ranks 109–120). So ALM-05 should report all 120 alarms that run N already produces (D-PERF-04), not only "20 and 60". (2) Pool raw samples across both vehicles and runs; never average per-vehicle percentiles. "Statistically inaccurate aggregation" is a named tail-latency pitfall (Treadmill 2016, abstract). The pass rule stays the spec's point p95; a CI that straddles the threshold is flagged "marginal" in ATP-08. *Strengthens:* ALM-05, ING-09, UI-01, ATP-08. *Effort:* S (inside R15 E3's script).

**E-8 Hazardous-confirmation dialog against habituation (UI only).** *Problem:* with two vehicles on one console, the likeliest hazardous error in nominal operation is the right command to the wrong vehicle. A confirm dialog that is clicked through every time habituates. *Evidence:* ground-operator errors cluster in **nominal** procedures, driven by inattention and inadequate MMI (Albrecht 2012, abstract). Warnings habituate (Anderson 2016, abstract). Inhibitive attractors, including retyping the salient field, "significantly reduced" risky choices even after habituation, at the cost of a few seconds (Bravo-Lillo 2013 pp. 2, 8, 11). Layered defences (Reason 2000). *Change:* the confirm `<dialog>` repeats vehicle, command, args and hazard text, and the Confirm button stays disabled until the operator **types the target vehicle ID**. API, token and audit stay unchanged (D-CMD-06 still enforces). Test: `e2e/hazardous-confirm.spec.ts` (wrong vehicle typed → Confirm disabled). The evidence comes from security dialogs, not spacecraft consoles, so it is labelled `default` in assumptions.md and the owner may drop it. *Strengthens:* CMD-03, OPS persona. *Effort:* S.

**E-9 Swarm the property suite over tuning values.** *Problem:* P3/P4 run with the §3.3 limits, so the engine could be correct only at N = 3 and the default deadbands. *Evidence:* FoundationDB randomises tuning parameters so "specific performance tuning values do not accidentally become necessary for correctness", and uses swarm testing, a random subset of fault kinds per run (Zhou 2021 p. 9). *Change:* the P3/P4/P5 generators draw `persistence` 1..5, `hysteresis_pct` from the valid range above the floor, ROC window 0.5–2 s, and a random subset of fault kinds per example (Hypothesis `st.sets`). Same 200/10 000 example budgets. Draw from the existing generators (`st.sampled_from`, `st.integers`), not new hand-written ones, which practitioners find costly (Goldstein 2024 p. 9). *Strengthens:* GS-EXT-03, D-ALM-10/11. *Effort:* S.

## 3. Design claims the evidence weakens

1. **R8 §Findings: "Chattering is the main cause of alarm overload."** Wang et al. name chattering as one of **four** main causes (p. 1). For floods they say: "the true reason that 'loss incidents frequently involved the operator being overloaded with alarm floods' … is that too many correct alarms arise in a short time period" (p. 7). → Say "one of four main causes, listed first".
2. **R8 §5: "Flood control comes only from per-param aggregation, deadband and persistence."** Those tools remove *false* alarms (Gyasi & Wang p. 2). Floods are mostly *correct* alarms from propagation (Wang p. 7), and rationalization does not control floods (Beebe, abstract). → At 2 × 9 parameters this is acceptable, but say "Nyx has no flood control; E-3 measures floods".
3. **D-ALM-03 "series design in the literature."** The structure is supported (Gyasi & Wang p. 2; Wang & Chen p. 4). The *values* are not: EEMUA delays are 15 s for pressure and 60 s for temperature (Izadi p. 4), and Wang & Chen's default is m = 20 s (p. 4), against Nyx's 0.3 s. N = 3 is a spec value (ALM-03/05). → Cite the spec for N and let E-2 justify it.
4. **R8: delay timers *and deadbands* lower false alarms "significantly".** Izadi uses "significantly" for delay only, and calls deadband "not as effective as filtering or time delay" (p. 6). The 15 % deadband is right for ALM-02 noise near a limit (small-deviation alarms, Gyasi p. 2), but it costs a 13.5 s clear after `isolate_valve` (§3.4). E-2 reports that cost.
5. **D-UI-07 "Why: ISA-101 high-performance HMI".** The peer-reviewed operator study found (Errington 2005) tested a whole human-centred interface, not grey-for-nominal alone. The only colour-vs-grey experiment found (Le Blanc et al. 2025, about 10 operators) shows better alarm detection with dull components (p. 15) but faster red/green status reading, and the authors stress low statistical power (p. 16). No study of stale-data indication (D-UI-05/06) was found among the free sources. → Keep the decision (UI-03 requires grey). Cite the spec, ISA-101 and Le Blanc 2025 p. 15 (alarm detection), and make no broader performance claim.
